# Two Small Steps: teacher card

**For:** ages 7 to 12, in pairs. **Time:** 15 minutes. **Needs:** one computer or tablet per pair with a web browser. No accounts, no installation.
**Link:** https://royito06.github.io/NasaSpaceApps2026/play/

## Goal

Each child can explain one real constraint of living on Mars: **a job that protects the crew today can cost power, oxygen or food tomorrow**, and the crew has to decide together because Earth's answer arrives too late.

## Plan

| Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Pairs choose "2 players". One child is **Engineering**, the other **Protection**. Tell them: "You each see half of the problem. Say what you see out loud." |
| 2–5 | **Level 1, the Moon.** Drive like Apollo 15. Ask: *"Why did Apollo 15 never drive farther than it could walk back?"* |
| 5–11 | **Level 2, Mars, sols 30 and 31.** Before choosing, each child reads their own report aloud. They **predict** (more or less radiation?), **choose together**, then see what it cost the next morning. |
| 11–15 | **Debrief** (below). Optional: a free mission for pairs who finish early. |

## Debrief questions

1. What did your crew give up to get what it chose?
2. What did your partner know that you didn't?
3. Why couldn't Earth help in time?
4. What did yesterday's choice change today (oxygen tanks, lettuces)?
5. Would the same job win with a different storm? Why not?

## Words to explain on the way

- **Sol:** a day on Mars, 24 hours and 39 minutes.
- **Radiation:** invisible energy from the Sun that adds up and never goes back down; a shelter blocks most of a storm.
- **Microgravity:** where everything floats, so muscles get little work.

## How to see progress

Open **Crew logbook** on the mission screen. It counts missions played, free missions with goals met, predictions right, and how often the crew read both reports before choosing. **Copy as text** gives a summary for your notes. Everything stays on the device: nothing is sent anywhere, and **Clear logbook** erases it.

## Honest notes for the teacher

- Storms in the game come far more often than on real Mars, so that something happens in a short session. The game labels this as a game rule.
- The mouse study in the game is about mice, not astronauts.
- This is an independent student project, not endorsed by NASA. It has not yet been tested in a classroom; your observations are welcome.
