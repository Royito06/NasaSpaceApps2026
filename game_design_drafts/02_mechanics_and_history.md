# 02 · Mechanics and the real missions behind them

Each mechanic is a decision with a cost, inspired by something that really happened. Missions marked *(story)* are inspiration only, not data used in the game.

## In the minimal demo

| Mechanic | The decision | Real inspiration | NASA data behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earth delay** | Wait for Earth's advice (it arrives too late) or decide as a crew now | **Apollo 13** (1970): the ground team designed the CO₂ fix and the crew built it, but that was possible because the Moon is ~1.3 s away. **NASA CHAPEA** (2023–24 and 2025–26, a year-long Mars simulation *on Earth*): delays up to 22 min each way, plus a two-week communication blackout | Earth–Mars and Earth–Moon distances (NASA NSSDCA fact sheets) |
| **Split-information roles** | Each role shares what only it can see, then they agree | NASA trains astronauts in **expeditionary skills**: self-care, team care, teamwork and group living, communication, leadership/followership (NASA behavioral health presentation, 2026) | NASA Human Research Program: **teamwork** and **working without Earth's help** are top-rated (5×5) risks for Mars operations |
| **Storm: shelter vs power** | Spend the work slot on protection or on power | **Curiosity RAD** (2012–13) measured radiation on the surface of Mars. **Artemis I** (2022): Orion has a storm shelter designed to keep a big solar storm under 150 mSv | Mars surface dose; NASA's storm shelter example; DONKI storm events |

## Next version (after the demo works)

| Idea | The decision | Real inspiration | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rover range ("walkback")** | Drive farther for a better science site, or stay close enough to walk home if the rover breaks | **Apollo 15** (1971, first lunar rover). Astronaut Jim Irwin: walkback limits "defined our maximum distance from the Lunar Module… so we'd have the maximum amount of oxygen and water to get back… if the car did fail" | **Matches the challenge's own example almost word for word**: "trade oxygen reserves for an extra research opportunity". Quote it as Irwin's account (Apollo Lunar Surface Journal), not an official report |
| **Landing site** | Land near useful equipment or near the science target | **Apollo 12** (1969): pinpoint landing so Conrad and Bean could walk to the Surveyor 3 probe | A good Moon-level opening choice |
| **Greenhouse** | Plant now (costs water and power today, food in weeks) or rely on supplies | **Veggie** on the ISS: astronauts grew and ate lettuce. Rodolfo Neri Vela (Mexico, 1985) brought tortillas to space menus *(story)* | Real crops take 25+ days, so a short demo starts with plants already growing |
| **Crew wellbeing** | Skip rest or exercise to finish a job today, and pay for it later | **Skylab 4** (1973–74): crew workload clashed with the ground's schedule (don't call it a "strike"). **Twins Study**, **Mars-500** *(story)* | NASA OSDR: mice on the ISS lost about 18% of a leg muscle in ~33 days. Show strength, never weight |
| **Dust storm and power** | Save power when the sun dims, or keep systems running | **Opportunity** (2018): a global dust storm blocked the sun and the solar-powered rover went silent | We still need a sourced number for power lost |
| **Moon tutorial** | The same choices, but Earth answers almost live | **Artemis I** (2022) radiation data; **Artemis II** (Apr 2026, crewed lunar flyby) | Currently just a 10-second card |

## Ideas we dropped and why

- **Hubble and James Webb:** great missions, but they're telescopes. No outpost or crew trade-off.
- **Chang'E 4 Moon radiation data:** not used, because of NASA's eligibility rule on China.
