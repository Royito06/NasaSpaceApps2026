# 04 · Design rules and open questions

## Rules we committed to

**From the official challenge**
- **Ages 7–12.** Plain words; explain "sol", "radiation dose" and "low gravity" the first time they appear.
- **No free choice.** Every option costs something.
- **2–3 core resources at most.** The demo shows only **Power** and **Radiation exposure**.
- **Real NASA data, honestly labelled.** Never present archived data as live, and show sources in the game.
- **Browser-playable,** with no download and no account.

**Calm cooperation**
- Difficulty comes from **coordinating roles**, never from speed. The Earth delay is the only timer, and speed is never scored.
- The player sets the pace with an "Advance" button. At most one event per sol, and never two alarms at once.
- No flashing more than 3 times a second, no red flashing alarms, sound off by default, and a reduced-motion option.
- Pause and save are always visible. No badges, streaks or notifications.
- Failure is explained, never punishing. Show what happened and why, then let them retry.

**Honesty**
- Say "an independent educational game inspired by NASA's expeditionary skills". **Never** imply NASA endorses it, and never use the NASA logo.
- **Kids' privacy:** no names, no health data, no recordings.
- **About food:** say "the crew eats with less appetite and loses strength". Never mention weight.

## Open questions: this is where we need you

1. **The numbers of the core decision.** What starting Power and Exposure values, and how much does each choice move them, so that *neither choice always wins*? Run both branches on paper and show the results.
2. **What makes it a real dilemma for a 7-year-old?** For example: if power stays low, the greenhouse light goes off tonight and the kids can see it happen. What visible, kid-sized consequence goes with each choice?
3. **Replay variety without randomness feeling unfair.** For example, change the storm strength between runs (using different real DONKI storms) or how damaged the power is, so the best choice changes and kids have to explain why.
4. **Two players on one device.** How do we stop one confident kid from doing everything? Each role has information only it can see, and the decision needs both to "sign off".
5. **The debrief.** The three questions are drafted. How do they work for kids who can't read well yet: pictures, voice, choosing from options?
6. **Next mechanic after the demo.** Our favourite is **Apollo 15's walkback rule** (rover range vs oxygen to walk home). Do you agree, or is the greenhouse or the dust storm better?
7. **Anything we're overcomplicating.** Say it.
