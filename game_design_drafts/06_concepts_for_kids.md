# 06 · Turning hard concepts into things kids can do

Checked against the official challenge text (`../CHALLENGE.md`). Goal: a 7–12-year-old **walks away able to explain a real constraint**, not just having played.

## Gaps against the challenge (fix in the full demo)

1. **Life support must be in the main trade-off.** The challenge's key sentence: *"a choice that saves power today might cost oxygen tomorrow."* The real link: **the machines that make oxygen and recycle water run on electricity** (as on the ISS). Low power today means less oxygen tomorrow.
2. **Effects must carry over time:** at least **3 sols**, so today's choice shows up tomorrow.
3. **Add one science goal** that costs oxygen or power (e.g. collect a rock sample farther away). The challenge's example: *"trade oxygen reserves for an extra research opportunity."* Simplest form of the Apollo 15 "walkback" idea.
4. Food production: a greenhouse where you sow today and eat later.
5. Classroom flex: a one-page teacher card (3 discussion questions, a 15-minute plan).

## Concept → how a kid gets it → what they do → what they see

| Hard concept | Kid-sized explanation | Action | Visible feedback | Debrief question |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Communication delay** | A walkie-talkie so slow that the answer arrives after you've decided | Ask Earth, or decide as a crew | Earth's reply arrives late: "a message takes 11 minutes each way today" (real, JPL Horizons) | Why couldn't Earth help in time? |
| **Radiation dose** | Invisible sunbeams that add up like coins in a piggy bank; one day on Mars ≈ 33 chest X-rays | Reinforce the shelter | The exposure bar fills more slowly; units behind a "?" button | What made the bar fill faster? |
| **Shielding** | An umbrella against a sun storm, built *before* the storm | Spend the work slot on the shelter | The storm arrives (a real NASA DONKI event, archived); the crew inside is safer | What did building the shelter cost us? |
| **Power budget** | The base's battery runs everything: lights, air, heat | Repair power, or leave it low | Lights dim; next sol the oxygen machine slows | What did the low battery do the next day? |
| **Life support** | Machines make the air and clean the water, and they need electricity | (through the power choice) | The oxygen bar drops a little next sol | Where does the base's air come from? |
| **Food production** | Plants take weeks: sow today, eat later | Spend water and power on the greenhouse | Plants grow over the sols; fresh food lifts crew mood (game rule) | Why plant before you're hungry? |
| **Sol** | A Mars day, 40 minutes longer than ours | The "Advance" button | Counter: Sol 1 → 2 → 3 | — |
| **Low gravity / strength** | Without much gravity, muscles shrink like with a cast on (NASA OSDR mouse data) | Spend a slot on exercise | Crew strength holds | Why do astronauts exercise every day? |
| **Teamwork** | Each of you sees half the picture | Tell each other, then decide together | Better outcome when both roles' info is used (see the balance simulation) | What did your partner know that you didn't? |

## Language rules
- Explain each term the first time it appears, with an icon and one short sentence (optional voice).
- Hide technical units (mSv, kW) behind a "?" button for older kids, teachers and judges.
- Label numbers as **Real NASA data** (with the source) vs **Game rule**.
